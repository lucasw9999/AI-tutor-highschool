### The 2026 FRQs — the newest official Precalc anchor (verified 12 August 2026)

**[`ap26-frq-precalculus.pdf`](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap26-frq-precalculus.pdf)** — HTTP 200, ~294 KB. Cover reads **"2026 AP Precalculus Free-Response Questions."** Verbatim structure: **`SECTION II PART A` · `TIME – 30 MINUTES`** — "2 free-response questions," **graphing calculator required, "Make sure your calculator is in radian mode"** — then **Section II Part B, 30 minutes, no calculator**, 2 questions. (That 30 + 30 confirms the "was ≤2026" row in `2027-exam-changes.md`; his sitting gets **35 + 35**.)

**It is the same four task types, for a third straight year** — so the whole FRQ plan in `exam-strategy-and-scoring.md` is confirmed, not guessed:

| Q | Type | What the 2026 question actually asked |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Function Concepts *(calc)* | Graph of an **increasing** function `f` on **`−3 ≤ x ≤ 3`** plus a logarithmic `g`. (A) `h = g∘f`: find `h(2)`; find all `x` with `f(x) = 3`. (B) solve `g(x) = −1.5` as a decimal; **state the behavior of `g` as x decreases toward 1 in limit notation**. (C) is `f` invertible — **justify from points on the graph**. |
| **2** | Modeling a Non-Periodic Context *(calc)* | **Car bought at the end of 2019 (`t = 0`)**, worth **27.2** thousand at `t = 1` and **14.8** thousand at `t = 6`; model `V(t) = ab^t`. (A) write the two equations, then find `a` and `b` as decimals. (B) **average rate of change** `t = 1 → 6`, use it to estimate `V(3)`, then argue the secant estimate `A(t) > V(t)`. (C) derive a **domain limitation** from the car being donated at 2 thousand dollars. |
| **3** | Modeling a Periodic Context *(no calc)* | A **circular waterwheel turning counterclockwise**, **1 revolution per 10 seconds**; at `t = 0` point `W` is **6 feet directly above the center**. (A) give possible `(t, h(t))` for five labeled points on an **unscaled, axis-free** sinusoid. (B) fit `h(t) = a·sin(b(t + c)) + d`. (C) positive/negative + increasing/decreasing, then **concavity and whether the rate of change is increasing or decreasing**. |
| **4** | **Symbolic Manipulation** *(no calc)* | Pure algebra: solve an exponential (`g(x) = e^{2x}`) and a logarithmic (`h(x) = log₂(5x)`) equation on their domains; **rewrite one expression in the form `7^(…)`** and another **so that `sin x` appears exactly once with no other trig functions**; solve a **tangent** equation for all outputs of 1 on `[0, 2π)`. |

**Why this matters to Lucas:** it's the **direct analogue of the 2026 CSA released FRQ** the CSA side already runs as its official mock — the newest official artifact for this subject, from the same course he sits. **Content-aligned for May 2027**; only counts and timing change. Two practice notes, both from the table above:
- **Q4 is the symbolic-manipulation question again** — the lowest-scoring FRQ two years running (**mean 1.28/6 in 2024, 1.93/6 in 2025**). A third consecutive year of the same shape is the strongest confirmation in this file that no-calculator symbolic work is the 4→5 lever.
- **When you run the 2026 Q2, skip Part B(iii)** (the `A(t) > V(t)` secant argument) — `2027-exam-changes.md` records it as **removed** for 2027, with Part C expanded instead. 2026 Q1's Part C is the invertibility justification that gets the minor 2027 tweak.

⚠️ **2026 has no scoring guidelines yet** (`ap26-sg-precalculus.pdf` → 404, re-confirmed 12 August 2026). So run it as a **timed Section II under real conditions**, then grade it with the 2024/2025 rubrics for the matching task type — a self-award on the 2026 set is **not** officially calibrated.

## Exam-day mechanics (rehearse these)
- **Hybrid digital format:** you answer **multiple-choice in the Bluebook app** and **view the free-response prompts in Bluebook**, but you **handwrite your FRQ answers in a paper booklet** that gets collected and scored. **Rehearse the app half for free right now** via the ungated Bluebook test preview linked above — no join code needed.
- **Rehearse the handwriting format:** College Board posts a **"Sample Free-Response Booklet" PDF** (`ap-precalculus-hybrid-sample-free-response-booklet.pdf`) — practice writing full FRQ solutions in that layout so exam day isn't the first time.
- **Calculator (a graphing calculator is REQUIRED on the calculator parts — I-B and II-A):** an approved **physical graphing calculator** satisfies it, **and so does** the **built-in Desmos** in Bluebook. **Rehearse with Desmos** (it's guaranteed to be there): graphing, finding zeros/intersections, and **regressions** (linear/quadratic/exponential/sinusoidal) for the modeling FRQ. Know its limits — it won't do your algebra or write your justification. *(Practice on the free **desmos.com** calculator; on exam day you use the **built-in Bluebook Desmos** — same tool, not the web/app version.)*
- Bring **No. 2 pencils**; know your school's start time and what to bring (College Board's "What to Bring" list).
- **Sessions and start times (verified 12 August 2026):** College Board now labels sittings **Session 1 / Session 2**, "replacing the former morning and afternoon designations," "to prevent the disclosure of secure exam content across time zones." In the lower 48 the start times are unchanged: **Session 1 = 8 a.m. local, Session 2 = 12 p.m. local.** **AP Precalculus is Session 1, Tuesday 11 May 2027** (morning); **AP CSA is Session 2, Wednesday 12 May 2027** (afternoon) — so the two exams are about **30 hours apart, not the ~24 that "back-to-back" suggests.** That leaves Tuesday afternoon and Wednesday morning: use them for CSA, not for more Precalc. A per-school start-time lookup tool is "coming this fall" — until then confirm the exact local start time with the AP Coordinator.

## What's OUT of scope — don't study these (save time)
Verified from the CED Course-Framework notes + the Fall-2026 Clarification PDF. None of these appear on the AP exam:
- **(1.1)** Discriminating **open vs. closed intervals** for increasing/decreasing.
- **(1.5)** The **rational root theorem** and **Descartes' rule of signs**; factoring by hand is limited to common-factor + quadratic rules.
- **(1.11)** **Partial-fraction decomposition**; **synthetic division** is not required (polynomial long division suffices).
- **(2.1)** **Series sums** (arithmetic/geometric) — only *sequences* (nth term) are tested.
- **(2.14)** Contextual interpretation of the constants in a logarithmic model `a + b·logₙ(x)`.
- **(2.15)** **Semi-log:** log scale on the **y-axis for exponential data only** — log-x and log-log linearization are out.
- **(3.12)** **Half-angle identities** and **tangent sum/difference identities** (and any identity not listed) — only **Pythagorean + sum/difference + double-angle for sine & cosine** are assessed.
- **All of Unit 4** (parametric, vectors, matrices, conics) — class-only, never on the exam.
