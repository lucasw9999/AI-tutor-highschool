# AP Precalculus — Skills & Exam Weightings (where to spend your time)

**Why this matters:** the exam isn't just weighted by *topic* — it's weighted by **skill**. Knowing which skills carry the most points tells you exactly what to drill. (From the official CED Course Framework + Exam Information. Content/skills are unchanged for May 2027.)

## The 3 Mathematical Practices → 8 skills (with exam weight)
| Practice | Skill | Exam weight |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Procedural & Symbolic Fluency** *(~39–48% — the biggest)* | **1.A** Solve equations & inequalities | 14–17% |
| | **1.B** Express functions/expressions in equivalent forms | 9–13% |
| | **1.C** **Construct new functions** (transformations, compositions, inverses, regressions) | **15–19% ← single highest** |
| **2. Multiple Representations** *(~20–27%)* | **2.A** Identify information from a graph/table/equation/verbal | 14–17% |
| | **2.B** Construct equivalent representations | 6–9% |
| **3. Communication & Reasoning** *(~32–39%)* | **3.A** Describe characteristics of a function | 10–14% |
| | **3.B** Apply results / make conclusions | 9–13% |
| | **3.C** Support conclusions with reasoning/evidence | 13% |

## What this means for study priority (do in this order)
1. **1.C — Construct new functions** (highest-weighted skill): transformations, compositions, **inverses**, and **regressions**. Drill these the most.
2. **Practice 1 overall is ~40–48%** → **no-calculator symbolic fluency** (solving, rewriting, identities) is the biggest bucket. This is also why **FRQ Q4 (pure Practice 1) is the top 4→5 lever.**
3. **2.A (read representations) + 3.A/3.C (describe & justify)** are large too → practice *explaining* characteristics and **justifying** answers in words, not just computing.

## Per-FRQ skill map (what each FRQ scores — target the right skills)
| FRQ | Skills tested (points) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1 Function Concepts** | 1.A(1), 1.C(1), 2.A(2), 3.A(1), 3.C(1) | heavy on reading representations + describing |
| **Q2 Modeling Non-Periodic** | 1.B(1), 1.C(2), 3.B(1), 3.C(2) | *pre-2027 layout; restructured for May 2027 (Part B(iii) removed, C expanded) — skills similar, lots of justification* |
| **Q3 Modeling Periodic** | 1.C(2), 2.A(1), **2.B(2)**, 3.A(1) | **the ONLY FRQ that tests 2.B (construct equivalent representations)** — i.e., build/label the sinusoid |
| **Q4 Symbolic Manipulation** | 1.A(4), 1.B(2) | **purely Practice 1** — pure algebra/trig by hand; no reasoning-words needed, just correct symbolic work |

## MCQ content weighting (two official cuts — don't conflate them)
- **By function type:** General Functions 15–23% · Polynomial & Rational 20–25% · Exponential & Logarithmic 22–28% · **Trigonometric & Polar 30–35%**.
- **By unit:** Unit 1 30–40% · Unit 2 27–40% · **Unit 3 30–35%** · Unit 4 = 0% (not assessed).
- **Trig & Polar (Unit 3) is the single heaviest area either way** → give it the most reps.

> ⚠️ **Do not let SAT Math practice stand in for Unit 3.** The SAT Math ↔ Precalc overlap is real but **narrow — Units 1–2 only** (polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic ↔ SAT Advanced Math). **SAT Math does not test trig or polar at all**, so the heaviest Precalc unit gets **zero** benefit from SAT reps, and Precalc trig work earns nothing back on the SAT. Budget Unit 3 time **separately**, and never trade it away on a felt sense of overlap. (Full note: `practice-resources-and-exam-day.md` § "Cross-subject leverage.")
