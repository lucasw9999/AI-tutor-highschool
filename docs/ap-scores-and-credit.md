# AP Scores After the Exam: Cancel, Withhold, and What Credit Actually Buys

**For the parent.** Two decisions happen after the exam and before scores post in July — and only one of them can be undone. Facts below are verified directly from College Board's AP Students site on **12 August 2026**. Dates specific to the 2027 exam administration are not yet published (marked "expected" below) and should be rechecked in spring 2027.

## 1. Cancel vs. withhold — the decision has a deadline

| | **Cancel** | **Withhold** |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | Erases the score everywhere — including from the student's **own** record | Blocks the score from **one** named college; everyone else (the student included) still has it |
| Reversible? | **No.** "Once a score is canceled, it can't be reinstated." | **Yes.** Remove the withhold at any time, free |
| Cost | No fee for the service, but **the exam fee is not refunded** | $10 per score per recipient; sending it to that school later needs a new order (the standard $15 score-send fee) |
| 2026-exam deadline (published) | Request must be received by **June 15, 2026** | Withhold ordering opens **July 6, 2026** |
| Lucas's May 2027 exams | **Expected ~June 15, 2027** — College Board has not yet published a 2027-specific date; this is the same mid-June pattern, not a confirmed deadline | **Expected to open ~early July 2027** — same caveat, not yet published |

Source: apstudents.collegeboard.org "Cancel Scores" and "Withhold Scores" pages, retrieved 12 August 2026.

**Why this belongs on a checklist now, not in July:** the cancellation window for a given exam closes in mid-June and can't be reversed once used, so the decision has to be made before — not after — scores post. If there's any doubt about whether a particular college should see a score, withholding is the safer default: it's reversible, and undoing it is free, where undoing a cancellation is not possible at all.

## 2. AP Precalculus credit is inconsistent — and zero at UC

Credit policy is set school-by-school and changes from year to year. As of 12 August 2026:
- **University of California (systemwide):** "No credit is awarded for the Precalculus exam offered in 2024 and beyond" — at any score. (AP Computer Science A, by contrast, is worth 8 quarter / 5.3 semester units at UC for a 3 or higher.)
- **CSU and USC** both credit AP Precalculus at a 3 or higher (CSU: 3 units plus GE Area 2; USC: treated like Calculus AB, waives MATH 108).
- **MIT's** advanced-placement page doesn't mention Precalculus at all, and separately states no credit is given for the AP Computer Science exams at any score.

None of this generalizes — one UC campus's policy doesn't tell you another public or private school's policy, and it can change year to year. Where a 4 vs. a 5 matters is just as school-specific: at CSU, UT Austin, and Ohio State the two scores buy the same thing (the real cliff is 3 vs. 4, not 4 vs. 5); MIT is a counterexample on Calculus BC, where a 4 earns nothing and a 5 does. Don't assume the "4 or 5" target has the same payoff everywhere — check the specific school.
