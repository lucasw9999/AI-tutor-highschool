# Day-1 Diagnostic — English Placement Check (~30 min, for Lucas)

Not a test you can fail — it just shows the tutor where to start so it never wastes your time. The grade in this class is won on **analytical writing**, so most of this checks that. Do it in your tutor (paste it in) or on your own, then tell the tutor how it went.

---

## Part A — Writing (the important part)
**A1.** In ONE sentence, what's the difference between a **summary** statement and a **thematic argument** about a book?

**A2.** Here is a weak thesis: *"In Night, Elie Wiesel shows how the Holocaust was really bad and many people suffered."*
Rewrite it as a **thematic argument** (something arguable about *why/what it means*, not a summary).

**A3.** Embed this quote into a sentence of your own (don't just drop it in after a comma):
> evidence to embed: `"the curves of her body continually smouldering"`
(Make up a topic sentence context; the point is the *blending*.)

**A4.** Read this commentary and say what's wrong with it (hint: the "black out the quote" test):
> "This shows that people hide their pain. A lot of people pretend everything is fine when really it is not. This is very common in society."

## Part B — Reading & analysis
**B1.** Pick ANY book you've read. Name one **theme** (as a claim, not a word) and one piece of **evidence** for it.

**B2.** What's the difference between a **character's trait** and a **character's arc**?

## Part C — Devices & vocab (quick)
**C1.** Define any 3: *irony, foil, soliloquy, metaphor, alliteration.*

**C2.** Use ONE of these SAT words in a sentence that shows you know it: *ubiquitous, pernicious, diatribe, implacable.*

## ✅ What your answers reveal (the tutor uses this to set your levels)
- **A1/A2 (thesis):** the single most important skill. If summary vs. argument is fuzzy, start at `guides/essay-writing.md` → "Thesis." (This is the usual B→A gap.)
- **A3 (embedding):** if you dropped the quote in with a comma, flag "embedding quotes" 🟡.
- **A4 (commentary):** the answer is *it's too generic — black out the quote and you can't tell which book it's about; it also uses RIP words ("this shows," "a lot," "very," "in society").* If you missed that, "commentary depth" + "RIP words" are early targets.
- **B1/B2 (analysis):** shows whether you can talk about a text beyond plot.
- **C1/C2 (devices/vocab):** quick gauge; sets where to start the devices guide and the vocab habit.
