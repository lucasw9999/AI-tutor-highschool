# How To Use This — Plain Instructions (for Lucas)

This is your project, Lucas. You run it. Here's exactly how it works.

## The big idea (read this once)
**You do NOT read a textbook.** No chapters, no 100 pages. You **solve problems**, and the AI tutor explains *only the one thing you're stuck on*, in 1–2 sentences, exactly when you hit it. Learning happens by doing + getting unstuck — not by reading ahead. If a session ever turns into "read this long explanation," it's doing it wrong; tell it **"just give me the next problem."**

## What you actually do (it's small)
- **One-time:** set up the Project (5 min, steps below).
- **Each session:** solve problems with the tutor. At the end, it prints a `TRACKER UPDATE` block — paste it into `mastery-tracker.md` so next time it remembers where you are. ~30 seconds.
- **Weekly:** glance at `mastery-tracker.md` to watch concepts turn 🟢. You don't need anyone to teach you — the AI does that. You just keep showing up.

## How a session runs
**You talk directly to the AI.** You read the problem, type your answer/code in the chat, ask for a hint if you're stuck. The AI does all the teaching and grading.

---

## Time: how much, how often
- **30–45 minutes a day, 5–6 days a week.** That's the sweet spot.
- **Once a day, not twice.** One focused session beats two — better attention, less burnout. (If you're *into it* one day and want a second short round, fine — but never force it.)
- **Optional add-on:** 5 minutes of flashcards (the rote traps like `==` vs `.equals()`). Tiny, high-value, skippable.
- **Consistency > length.** 35 min daily for 6 weeks crushes 3-hour weekend cram sessions.

### What that buys you (summer 2026)
At ~40 min/day, ~5 days/week, you'll get through **Java syntax + Unit 4 (the biggest unit)** over the summer — so when school starts in August, the class is *review*, not first contact. That single fact is what makes the whole year low-stress.

---

## What ONE session actually looks like (step by step)
1. Open the Project and type **"let's continue"** (very first time: **"let's start"**).
2. Tutor reads the tracker and says one line: *"Last time you nailed for-loops. Today: arrays. Here's problem 1."*
3. **Tutor gives a problem.** You type your answer/code right in the chat.
4. **Stuck?** Type **"hint."** Tutor gives a nudge (not the answer). Try again. Still stuck → another hint. Only after two real tries does it show the full solution.
5. Got it right → tutor gives **2–3 more of the same kind** to lock it in → moves up.
6. After ~30–40 min the tutor says *"good stopping point"* and prints a **`TRACKER UPDATE`** block.
7. Paste that block into `mastery-tracker.md`. Done.

That's the entire loop. Solve → get unstuck → repeat → save progress.

## First 5 minutes — setup (do this once)
1. Open **Claude** → create a **Project** (or a Custom GPT in ChatGPT).
2. Paste the contents of **`tutor-prompt.md`** into the Project's instructions/system prompt.
3. Upload **`csa-coverage-map.md`** and **`mastery-tracker.md`** as Project files.
4. Type **"let's start."** The first session is a short **diagnostic** (placement check) — no studying yet, it just figures out what you already know so it never wastes your time.

---

## The first three days (so it's not confusing)
- **Day 1:** Diagnostic. Answer a handful of quick questions + try one easy task. Tutor sets your starting levels. ~30 min. (Optional: skim the Python→Java cheat-sheet — 15 min, once.)
- **Day 2:** Tutor starts you on the weakest basics it found. Pure problem-solving.
- **Day 3:** More of the same, climbing the ladder. By now the rhythm clicks and you run it solo.

After day 3, just open it and type "let's continue" each day, then paste the update. That's it.

---

## How you'll know it's working
- The tracker fills with 🟢 over time (you can literally see progress).
- "Stuck → hint → got it" cycles get shorter on topics you've seen.
- **Checkpoint:** every 2–3 weeks, try a real released FRQ; the score climbing is the proof.

## If it ever feels off
- Too much reading/explaining → **"just give me problems."**
- Too easy/boring → **"make these harder"** (the tutor scales difficulty).
- Too hard/frustrating → **"give me an easier one first."** The tutor keeps it at the edge of your ability — challenging but doable.
