# Adaptive Strategy, Pacing & Test-Taking — Digital SAT

**Why this matters for your score:** The Digital SAT is *adaptive* — your Module 1 performance unlocks (or locks) your score ceiling for the rest of the section. Master the mechanics below and you stop leaving points on the table to dumb pacing and panic. This is the highest-leverage, lowest-content part of your whole prep: it's points you earn without learning a single new formula.

> **How to use this pack:** It's question-driven. Do the drill, then check the worked answer. Each answer teaches the *rule* you needed. Don't read it like a textbook — DO it, miss, learn the rule, move on. Re-do any set you scored below 80% on a week later.

## 0. The 60-Second Map (memorize this)

| | Reading & Writing | Math |
|---|---|---|
| Questions | 54 (two 32-min modules) | 44 (two 35-min modules) |
| Time | 64 min | 70 min |
| Per question | **~1.2 min** (71 sec) | **~1.6 min** (95 sec) |
| Calculator | **No** | **Yes — entire section** (built-in Desmos + your own approved one + reference sheet) |
| Format | All multiple choice, one short passage each (~25–150 words) | ~75% multiple choice, ~25% grid-in (student-produced) |

- **Total:** 98 questions, ~2h14m, **10-min break** between RW and Math.
- **Score:** 400–1600 (RW 200–800 + Math 200–800). **Your target: 1500+.**
- **No guessing penalty. Ever. Never leave a question blank.**
- Each section = **2 modules**. Module 1 = mixed difficulty (same for everyone). Module 2 difficulty + your max possible score is set by how you did in Module 1.
- ~2 questions per module are **unscored "pretest" items** you can't identify. Treat *every* question as if it counts — because you can't tell which don't.

## 1. How Adaptive (Multistage) Testing Actually Works

> **Why this matters:** It's NOT item-by-item adaptive like some computer tests. It adapts ONCE per section, *between* the two modules. That single hinge point is where your section is won or lost.

**The mechanic, plainly:**
1. **Module 1 = the routing stage.** Everyone gets the same broad mix of easy/medium/hard. This is your audition.
2. Bluebook scores Module 1, then routes you to **Module 2 (harder)** or **Module 2 (easier)**.
3. **Harder Module 2 = access to the full score range** (the path to 700s/800s). **Easier Module 2 caps your score** well below 800 no matter how perfectly you do it.
4. RW routing is independent of Math routing. Each section has its own Module 1.

**What this means for your strategy (the takeaways that change behavior):**
- **Module 1 accuracy is the single most valuable thing on the test.** A missed easy question in Module 1 hurts far more than a missed hard one in Module 2, because it can route you down into a capped second module.
- In Module 1, **bank the easy and medium questions first** — those are the routing currency. Don't burn 3 minutes heroically on one hard question and then rush (and miss) three easy ones.
- Once you're *in* Module 2, the route is locked. Now it's pure "get as many right as possible." No more strategic routing to worry about — just accuracy and pacing.
- You **cannot go back to Module 1 after it ends.** Each module is timed and sealed separately.

### Drill 1 — Adaptive logic (answer T/F or pick)
1. *(T/F)* Getting a hard Module 1 question wrong is worse than getting an easy one wrong.
2. *(T/F)* If you nail Module 2, you can still hit 800 even if you were routed to the easier Module 2.
3. *(T/F)* The Math Module 1 result affects which RW module you get.
4. You have 90 seconds left in Module 1 and an unsolved hard question plus two untouched easy ones. What do you do?
   - (A) Finish the hard one — it's worth more.
   - (B) Lock in the two easy ones, then guess on the hard one.
5. *(T/F)* Pretest (unscored) questions change which Module 2 you get routed to.

**Worked answers — Drill 1**
1. **FALSE.** All questions in a module are weighted the same for *routing*; missing an *easy* one is worse because easy ones are the ones you're expected to get, and the routing reads your overall Module-1 accuracy. **Rule: protect easy/medium points first.**
2. **FALSE.** The easier Module 2 **caps your ceiling** — a perfect easier Module 2 still scores below a perfect harder one. **Rule: the ceiling is set in Module 1; Module 2 just fills in within that ceiling.**
3. **FALSE.** Sections are independent; each has its own Module 1 routing. **Rule: a bad RW doesn't doom Math, and vice-versa — reset your mindset at the break.**
4. **(B).** Two easy points > one hard point, and you never blank the hard one (no penalty). **Rule: in the routing module, harvest the sure points; never trade two likely-rights for one maybe-right.**
5. **FALSE.** Pretest items are unscored and don't affect routing — but you can't identify them, so play every question straight. **Rule: treat all questions as scored.**

## 2. Pacing — The Numbers and the Behavior

> **Why this matters:** Running out of time = blanks = lost points you could have guessed into. A 1500+ scorer almost never leaves a question unanswered. Pacing is a *trained habit*, not a hope.

**Your clock targets:**
- **RW: ~1.2 min/question** (27 questions per module in 32 min). Most RW questions should take **under a minute**; that banks time for the 2–3 dense ones (long passages, "main idea," paired-passage logic).
- **Math: ~1.6 min/question** (22 per module in 35 min). Easy algebra in 30–45 sec banks time for multi-step word problems and geometry.
- Bluebook shows a **countdown timer** (you can hide it) and **warns at 5 minutes left**. Use the 5-min warning as your "stop solving, start bubbling everything blank" trigger.

**The pacing rules of a 1500+ scorer:**
1. **Never spend more than ~2× the average on any single question.** If an RW question passes ~90 sec or a Math question passes ~3 min and you're not nearly done — flag it, pick your best guess, move on.
2. **First pass: collect the gimmes.** Go front to back doing every question you can solve quickly. Flag anything slow or uncertain.
3. **Second pass: the flagged ones.** Now you know how much time you have left, so you spend it where it pays.
4. **Final 5 min: zero blanks.** Make sure every question has *an* answer, even a blind guess.
5. **The Desmos trap:** Desmos can save huge time on Math (graph it, find intersections), but it can also eat 3 minutes if you over-engineer a problem you could do by hand. Use it as a tool, not a crutch.

### Drill 2 — Pacing math (compute it)
1. You're 18 questions into RW Module 1 with 9 questions left and 8 minutes on the clock. Are you ahead or behind pace, and by how much per question?
2. Math Module 2: you have 12 questions left and 15 minutes. What's your per-question budget, and is that tighter or looser than the standard Math pace?
3. You've spent 4 minutes on one Math problem and still aren't sure. What's the move?
4. RW: a question is taking you 2.5 minutes. What rule did you just break, and what do you do?
5. With the 5-minute warning flashing and 3 questions untouched, rank your actions.

**Worked answers — Drill 2**
1. 8 min ÷ 9 = **~53 sec each**. Standard RW pace is ~71 sec. You're slightly **behind** (you have less than the average per question), so speed up — but it's close, ~18 sec/question tight. **Rule: do the division, don't guess your pace by feel.**
2. 15 min ÷ 12 = **75 sec each**, vs. the 95-sec Math standard. That's **tighter** than standard — pick up the pace and lean on Desmos for anything graphable. **Rule: recompute your budget every time you check the clock.**
3. **Guess, flag, move on.** 4 min on one Math question is already over 2× budget; the opportunity cost is 2–3 other questions. **Rule: cap any single question at ~2× the average.**
4. You broke the **~2× cap** (90 sec for RW). Eliminate what you can, pick the best remaining choice, flag it, move on. **Rule: a flagged best-guess beats a perfect answer you never reach.**
5. (1) Put *some* answer on all 3 untouched (no blanks), (2) then if seconds remain, upgrade the most-gettable one. **Rule: blanks are the only truly wasted question — kill all blanks first.**

## 3. Guessing — There Is No Penalty, So There Is No Excuse

> **Why this matters:** The old SAT subtracted points for wrong answers. The Digital SAT does **not**. A blank and a wrong answer score identically (zero) — but a guess has a real chance of being right. Leaving anything blank is strictly worse than guessing. This is free expected points.

**The rules:**
- **Every question gets an answer.** No exceptions.
- **Eliminate first, then guess.** On a 4-choice MC question, blind guessing ≈ 25% right. Cross out one wrong answer → ~33%. Cross out two → 50%. Elimination *multiplies* your guess value.
- **Grid-in (Math student-produced):** no choices to eliminate, but still **never leave blank** — put your best attempt (even a partial computation or a plausible number). Zero downside.
- **Letter-of-the-day for true blind guesses:** if you have 4 seconds and 3 untouched MC questions, pick one letter and slap it on all of them. It's faster than thinking and statistically fine.
- Use **"Mark for Review"** to flag a guess you'd revisit — but only *after* you've entered a guess answer, so it's never accidentally left blank.

### Drill 3 — Guessing EV (expected value)
1. Four MC questions, all blind-guessed. About how many do you expect to get right?
2. You eliminate 2 of 4 choices on a question. What's your probability of guessing right now?
3. A grid-in Math question stumps you completely. Blank it or guess?
4. You're choosing between leaving 2 questions blank vs. blind-guessing both. Which scores more *on average*, and why?
5. *(T/F)* On the Digital SAT, a wrong answer costs you more than a blank.

**Worked answers — Drill 3**
1. ~1 (4 × 25% = **1 expected correct**). One free point from pure noise. **Rule: blind guessing has positive expected value — always take it.**
2. **50%** (1 of the 2 remaining). **Rule: every elimination roughly doubles or better your odds — eliminate before you guess.**
3. **Guess.** No penalty; a plausible number could hit, a blank never can. **Rule: grid-ins still get a best-attempt answer, never a blank.**
4. **Blind-guessing both** scores more on average (~0.5 expected points vs. a guaranteed 0). **Rule: a blank's expected value is exactly zero; a guess's is always positive.**
5. **FALSE.** Wrong = blank = 0. **Rule: there's no penalty, so there's no reason to ever blank.**

## 4. Bluebook Tools — Use Every One

> **Why this matters:** Bluebook (the testing app) has built-in tools that save time and catch errors. Kids who don't practice with them on real Bluebook practice tests leave speed and accuracy on the table.

| Tool | What it does | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Mark for Review (flag)** | Flags a question + shows it on the review screen | Any question you guessed on or want a second look — *after* answering it |
| **Review screen** | End-of-module map of answered/flagged/blank questions | Last 2 min of every module: hunt for blanks and flags |
| **Annotate/highlight (RW)** | Highlight passage text + add notes | Long RW passages, "which choice supports…" questions |
| **Answer eliminator (strike-through)** | Cross out MC choices you've ruled out | Every MC question you're unsure on — makes elimination visual |
| **Built-in Desmos calculator** | Full graphing + scientific, toggle anytime in Math | Graphing, solving systems, finding intersections/zeros, checking work |
| **Reference sheet** | Formulas (areas, volumes, special triangles, etc.) provided in Math | When you blank on a formula — don't memorize what's already given |
| **Countdown timer** | On-screen clock; warns at 5 min | Glance every ~5–6 questions to recompute pace; hide it if it stresses you |
| **Zoom / line reader / contrast** | Accessibility/readability tools | If text density slows you down |

**Pro habits:**
- **Desmos is your Math superpower** — learn it cold *before* test day: type an equation → see the graph; type two equations → read off the intersection; use the table for data questions. It turns many "hard" algebra problems into 20-second graph reads.
- **The reference sheet means you don't memorize geometry formulas** — but you *do* need to know which formula to grab, fast.
- **Always answer, then flag.** Flagging a blank is a trap; flag only answered questions.

### Drill 4 — Tools (pick the tool)
1. A linear-system Math question ("where do these two lines meet?"). Fastest tool?
2. An RW question: "Which quotation best supports the student's claim?" with a 130-word passage. Best tool?
3. You finish a module with 90 seconds left. What screen do you open?
4. You can't remember the volume-of-a-cone formula on a Math question. What do you do?
5. You guessed on Q7 and want to revisit it. Correct sequence of actions?

**Worked answers — Drill 4**
1. **Built-in Desmos** — graph both equations, read the intersection point. Faster and less error-prone than algebra. **Rule: if it graphs, graph it.**
2. **Highlight/annotate** the passage — mark the claim, then match each quote choice against it. **Rule: use highlighting to anchor evidence questions.**
3. The **Review screen** — scan for any blanks (fix first) and flagged questions (upgrade if time). **Rule: end every module on the review screen.**
4. Open the **provided reference sheet** — it's there; don't waste memory or panic. **Rule: never memorize what the test hands you.**
5. **Answer Q7 with your best guess first → then Mark for Review → revisit on second pass.** **Rule: answer, then flag — never flag a blank.**

## 5. Section-by-Section Game Plan to 1500+

> **Why this matters:** 1500+ means roughly missing only a handful per section. That comes from a *repeatable process*, not from being smarter under pressure. Run the same play every time.

### Reading & Writing (target ~750+)
- Questions are **arranged easiest → hardest within a module** and grouped by type — so the back end is where the time-sinks live. Move briskly through the front.
- **Read the question first** for many RW items, then read the short passage with the task in mind.
- **Standard English Conventions** (grammar/punctuation) questions are fast, rule-based points — get them quick and bank time.
- **Module 1 priority:** accuracy on the easy/medium grammar + main-idea questions. Those route you up.
- First pass for gimmes, flag the dense inference/paired-passage ones, second pass with banked time, review screen at the end.

### Math (target ~750+)
- **Calculator allowed throughout** — but don't reach for Desmos when mental math or a quick hand-calc is faster. Desmos shines on graphing, systems, and data.
- **~25% are grid-ins** — no choices, so double-check your entry format (decimals/fractions both accepted; watch negative signs and rounding rules shown on screen).
- **Module 1 priority:** lock the Algebra and Problem-Solving/Data questions (the bulk of easy/medium points) before grinding on Advanced Math or Geometry/Trig hard items.
- Word problems (~30% of Math): translate to an equation first, *then* compute. Misreading the setup is the #1 error, not arithmetic.
- First pass gimmes, flag multi-steppers, Desmos-check anything graphable, review screen at the end.

### The whole-test routine
1. **Before each module:** breathe, remember Module 1 = routing (protect easy points).
2. **First pass:** harvest fast points, flag the rest.
3. **Mid-module clock checks:** recompute time ÷ questions-left every ~5–6 Qs.
4. **Second pass:** spend banked time on flags.
5. **Last 5 min:** kill all blanks, then upgrade flags.
6. **The 10-min break (between RW and Math):** reset completely. Bad RW feeling? Irrelevant — Math routes independently. Hydrate, breathe, restart.

### Drill 5 — Game-plan scenarios
1. RW Module 1, first pass: you hit a brutal paired-passage inference question at Q6. What do you do?
2. Math Module 1: you're routed to easier vs. harder Module 2 based on what?
3. You finish Math Module 1 feeling shaky. How should that affect Math Module 2?
4. It's the break. You think you bombed RW. What's the right mental move?
5. Grid-in: your answer is 0.6666… What do you enter?

**Worked answers — Drill 5**
1. **Flag it and move on** to collect the rest of the easy/medium points; come back on the second pass. A hard Q6 is not worth jeopardizing easy Q7–Q27. **Rule: routing currency = breadth of easy/medium correct, not depth on one hard item.**
2. Based on your **Module 1 performance (accuracy)** — nothing else. **Rule: Module 1 is the audition; treat every question in it as routing-critical.**
3. It **shouldn't** change your behavior — the route is already locked when Module 2 starts; just maximize accuracy and pace. **Rule: stop worrying about routing once Module 2 begins.**
4. **Let it go** — Math routes independently of RW; dwelling on RW only sabotages Math. Reset at the break. **Rule: each section is a clean slate.**
5. Enter **.6666 or 2/3** (grid-ins accept fractions; if decimal, fill all available digits — don't round short, e.g. ".66" may be marked wrong). **Rule: for repeating decimals, fill the entry completely or use the exact fraction.**

## 6. The 10 Rules (cut this out)

1. **Module 1 is everything** — protect easy/medium points; they set your ceiling.
2. **Never blank anything** — no penalty; always guess.
3. **Eliminate, then guess** — every cross-out raises your odds.
4. **Cap any question at ~2× the average time** — flag and move on.
5. **First pass gimmes, second pass flags** — in both sections.
6. **RW ~1.2 min/Q, Math ~1.6 min/Q** — recompute time-left ÷ questions-left at every clock check.
7. **Answer, *then* flag** — never flag a blank.
8. **Graph it in Desmos** when it graphs; don't over-engineer simple ones.
9. **Grab the reference sheet** — don't memorize given formulas.
10. **Review screen + zero blanks in the final 5 minutes** of every module.

> **Practice for real:** Do these as **timed Bluebook practice tests** (College Board's official app), not on paper. The adaptive routing, the timer, Desmos, and the flag/review tools only feel automatic if you've rehearsed them in the actual environment. Aim to make the *process* boring and automatic so your brain is free for the actual questions.
