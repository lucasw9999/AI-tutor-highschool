# Scoring & What 1500+ Requires

> **Why this matters for your score:** The Digital SAT is adaptive — your first module decides how high you can possibly go, and because hard questions are worth more, *which* questions you miss matters as much as *how many*. Understanding this turns "get everything right" into a concrete, winnable game plan.

---

## The 60-second version

- **Score = 400–1600.** Two sections, each **200–800**: Reading & Writing (RW) + Math. They're added together.
- **It's adaptive between modules.** Each section = **Module 1** (routing) → **Module 2** (easier or harder, based on M1). The module you're routed into sets your **score ceiling** for that section.
- **It's not a raw count.** Scores use **Item Response Theory (IRT)** + **equating** — harder questions carry more weight, so you never see "X out of 54 correct."
- **The 1500+ rule in one line:** You must be routed into the **harder Module 2 in BOTH sections.** Easier-path = capped around the mid-600s per section ≈ ~1300 ceiling. No exceptions.

---

## 1. How the scoring machine actually works

**Two sections, each on a 200–800 scale, added for a 400–1600 total.** RW and Math are scored completely independently — your Math performance never touches your RW score and vice-versa.

**Item Response Theory (IRT), not a raw count.** Under IRT, every question has its own difficulty and "weight." A hard question you get right is worth more than an easy one. Because forms differ and questions are weighted, **you will never be told how many you got right or wrong** — only the scaled score. This is why two students with the same number correct can get *different* scores.

**Equating.** College Board mathematically adjusts every form so a 620 means the same thing on a hard form as on an easy form, this year or next. You can't "get lucky" with an easy test — equating cancels that out.

**The takeaway for you:** Don't obsess over a single missed question. Obsess over (a) getting routed into the hard module, and (b) not missing the *easy* questions, which are nearly free points everyone at your level is expected to bank.

---

## 2. The adaptive ceiling — the single most important concept

Each section has **two modules of equal length**:

```
        MODULE 1 (everyone gets the same mix:
        easy + medium + hard — this is the ROUTER)
                       |
          do well     /  \    don't
                     /      \
          MODULE 2B          MODULE 2A
          (HARDER)           (EASIER)
          ceiling ~800       ceiling ~mid-600s
```

- **Module 1 is the gatekeeper.** It is NOT adaptive — same difficulty mix for everyone. Your performance here routes you.
- **Module 2 sets your real ceiling.** Routed to the **easier** Module 2A → the questions simply don't carry enough IRT weight to reach the top, so even a *perfect* easy module tops out around the **mid-600s** for that section. Routed to the **harder** Module 2B → the full 800 is on the table.
- **On the hard path, you can miss more and still score high** — because hard items are worth more, a few misses in the hard module cost less than you'd think.

> **Bottom line for 1500+:** ~750 average per section. That is *only* reachable on the **harder Module 2 in both sections.** Step 1 of your entire prep plan = **lock in the hard-module route by crushing Module 1.**

*(Note: the exact M1 cutoff to route into the hard module is not published by College Board and varies by form. Prep sources estimate "roughly two-thirds correct" on Module 1 as the rough threshold — treat that as a floor to clear comfortably, not a target to barely hit.)*

## 3. Roughly how many can I miss? (Realistic bands)

Each section has **50 scored RW** and **40 scored Math** questions. Here's where those come from, so you can check the arithmetic yourself: College Board's spec gives **Reading & Writing 25 operational + 2 pretest questions per module**, and **Math 20 operational + 2 pretest per module**. Two modules each, so RW = (25 + 2) × 2 = **54 total, 50 scored**, and Math = (20 + 2) × 2 = **44 total, 40 scored**. The pretest items don't count and you can't tell which they are — so treat every question as real.

**All numbers below assume you've been routed into the harder Module 2** and are *estimates* — College Board never publishes exact conversion tables, and they shift form-to-form. Use them as targets, not guarantees. The **% correct** columns are just the miss counts divided into the 50 and 40 scored totals above, so you can sanity-check any band yourself.

| Section score | RW — approx. misses allowed | RW — that's | Math — approx. misses allowed | Math — that's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **~800** | 0–2 of 50 | 96–100% | 0–1 of 40 | 98–100% |
| **~780** | ~3–4 of 50 | 92–94% | ~2 of 40 | ~95% |
| **~750** | ~5–7 of 50 | 86–90% | ~3–4 of 40 | 90–93% |
| **~700** | ~8–10 of 50 | 80–84% | ~5–6 of 40 | 85–88% |

**Putting it together for your targets (both on the hard path):**

| Total goal | Rough split | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **1500** | 750 RW + 750 Math | Miss ~5–7 RW **and** ~3–4 Math, total. Very achievable. |
| **1550** | 770 RW + 780 Math | Miss ~4 RW and ~2 Math. Tight but normal for a strong student. |
| **1600** | 800 + 800 | Near-flawless on both hard modules. |

**Reality check, in plain terms:** 1500+ does **not** require perfection. It requires being routed hard in both sections and then missing only a *handful* per section. Math is less forgiving (fewer scored questions → each miss costs more scaled points), so Math accuracy is where you protect your score hardest.

---

## 4. Where points are gained most efficiently

Spend your prep time in this order — this is the highest return on effort:

1. **Stop missing EASY/MEDIUM questions.** Inside each module, questions go easiest → hardest. The early ones are nearly-free points that everyone at 1500 level banks. A careless miss here is the most expensive mistake on the test — it can also cost you the hard-module route. **Slow down on the openers.**
2. **Guarantee the hard-module route (esp. Math Module 1).** Since M1 decides your ceiling, blowing M1 is catastrophic. Treat M1 like the real test — no warming up.
3. **Math > RW for marginal points.** Fewer scored Math questions means each correct answer moves the needle more. If you have one hour left to study, put it on your weakest Math domain.
4. **Attack the biggest Math domains.** Algebra (~35%, 13–15 Qs) and Advanced Math (~35%, 13–15 Qs) are **~70% of Math** — about 28 of the 40 scored questions. Mastering linear/quadratic/exponential systems and functions beats grinding the smaller Geometry/Trig (~15%, 5–7) or Data Analysis (~15%, 5–7) buckets.
5. **NEVER leave a blank.** No penalty for wrong answers. Every unanswered question = a guaranteed loss. Eliminate, then guess. On grid-ins, always put *something* plausible.
6. **RW: Standard English Conventions = free points.** Grammar/punctuation rules are finite and memorizable — unlike reading inference, you can get these to near-100% with rule drills. Highest accuracy-per-hour in RW.

---

## 5. Your concrete plan, summarized

- **Goal:** ~750/750 → 1500. Stretch: 770/780 → 1550.
- **Non-negotiable:** Hard Module 2 in BOTH sections. Clear the routing threshold *comfortably* by not missing easy/medium questions in Module 1.
- **Allowed misses (hard path):** ~5–7 RW + ~3–4 Math gets you to 1500. You have room — you do **not** need perfection.
- **Protect Math accuracy** (each miss costs more) and **mine grammar/conventions** in RW (cheapest points).
- **Answer everything.** Blanks are the only mistake with zero upside.

## Practice Set — test your scoring strategy

*Answer these before reading the worked answers. These check that you understand the* mechanics*, which is what makes the difference at 1500+.*

**Q1.** You finish RW Module 1 having rushed and carelessly missed 6 of the easy/medium opening questions, but nailed the 4 hardest. Why is this a worse outcome than missing 6 of the hardest questions instead?

**Q2.** Two students both answer exactly 40 of the 50 scored RW questions correctly. One scores 720, the other 660. Give the two most likely reasons their scores differ.

**Q3.** Your goal is 1500. You're consistently routed into the *easier* Math Module 2 and scoring 650 in Math even when you get nearly everything right. RW is a solid 760 on the hard path. Can you hit 1500 by just grinding the easy Math module to perfection? What must change?

**Q4.** On test day you have 90 seconds left in Math Module 2 and 3 unanswered grid-in questions you can't fully solve. What do you do, and why?

**Q5.** True or false: To score 1550, you need to answer all 98 questions correctly. Explain.

---

### Worked Answers

**A1.** The early, easy/medium questions are the ones *everyone at your level gets right* — missing them is the most expensive error because (a) IRT gives them little weight when you're *right*, but missing them still drags you down relative to your peers, and (b) Module 1 is the **router**: bombing easy questions can knock you into the *easier* Module 2, capping your whole section near the mid-600s no matter how well you do after. The 4 hard ones you nailed can't rescue a route you've already lost. **Lesson: never rush the openers — protect the route first.**

**A2.** (1) **Different modules** — the 720 student was almost certainly routed into the **harder Module 2** (worth more scaled points), while the 660 student was on the **easier path**, which caps near the mid-600s. (2) **Which questions they missed** — 40 of 50 means **10 misses each**, and under IRT missing 10 *hard* questions costs less than missing 10 *easy/medium* ones, so the distribution of those 10 misses differs. Same raw count, different scaled score — exactly why College Board never reports raw counts.

**A3.** **No.** The easier Module 2A has a hard ceiling around the mid-600s; a perfect easy module still can't break ~660-ish. 760 RW + ~650 Math ≈ 1410, short of 1500. **What must change: you have to get routed into the *harder* Math Module 2.** That means raising your **Module 1 Math** performance (don't miss easy/medium items; clear the routing threshold comfortably). All your prep energy should go to Math Module 1 accuracy, not grinding an easy module you've already maxed.

**A4.** **Put an answer in every blank — guess if needed.** There's no wrong-answer penalty, so a blank is a guaranteed zero while a guess has upside. For grid-ins, enter any plausible value (even a partial result or estimate). Three guesses beat three blanks every time. **Lesson: blanks are the only zero-upside move on the test.**

**A5.** **False.** 1550 needs roughly 770 RW + 780 Math, which on the **harder modules** allows about **~4 RW misses and ~2 Math misses** — you can drop a handful and still hit 1550. You do not need a perfect 98/98. (A perfect *raw* score is only required to guarantee a 1600.) **Lesson: aim for the hard route + low misses, not flawlessness.**
