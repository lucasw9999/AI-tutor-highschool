# 10th English — External Resources (every link resolved)

**Why this file exists:** until now this project had **no external resources at all**. Every other subject in this repo has a `reference/` folder full of official material; English had five study packs, six guides, and the only web addresses anywhere in it were `doi.org` *examples* inside the MLA citation formats. That's a gap, because two of the four things this class grades — **analytical essays** and **grammar exams** — have free, official, high-quality outside material sitting right there.

**Everything below was opened and checked on 12 August 2026.** Nothing here is a guess, a remembered URL, or a "should be at." If a link ever dies, don't reconstruct it — search the site.

## Why the AP Lit rubric is the one to actually use

Mrs. Pagtakhan grades analytical essays, and the class already runs **FRQ-style questions** (`../coverage-map.md`). The AP Lit rubric is an **official rubric for the exact skill she grades** — and it turns out to say the same things `guides/essay-writing.md` already teaches.

Here are the 2026 FRQ's four criteria, **verbatim**, against the guide that already teaches them:

| AP Lit says (verbatim) | This project already calls it |
|---|---|
| *"Respond to the prompt with a thesis that presents a defensible interpretation"* | **§2 The Thesis** — "clear, specific, **arguable** thematic argument that goes beyond summary" (`../guides/essay-writing.md`) |
| *"Select and use evidence to support your line of reasoning"* | **§4 The Body Paragraph** — ≥2 embedded quotes per paragraph |
| *"Explain how the evidence supports your line of reasoning"* | **§5 Commentary — the "Black Out the Quote" test** |
| *"Use appropriate grammar and punctuation"* | **`../guides/grammar.md`** + the RIP-words editing pass |

**So use it as a calibration source, not a curriculum:**

1. Write an essay for Mrs. P the normal way, using `essay-writing.md`.
2. Before you turn it in, score it against the **2024 Set 1 rubric** as if a College Board reader had it. Be harsh.
3. Wherever you can't award yourself the point, you've found the actual weakness — usually the "explain how the evidence supports" row, which is exactly the blackout test.

> **Two honest caveats.** (1) AP Lit is a **junior/senior** course; its passages are harder than sophomore English, so the *prompts* will feel steep. Ignore that — you're borrowing the rubric's *language*, not competing with AP students. (2) The AP rubric is **not** Mrs. P's rubric. Hers is the one that decides your grade, and it's reproduced in `essay-writing.md`. Where they disagree, **she wins.** Use AP Lit as a second opinion that happens to be official.

## ⚠️ The filename trap, recorded so nobody repeats it

AP English Literature files do **not** follow one clean pattern, and constructing a URL by analogy fails:

- Scoring guidelines carry a **`-set-1` / `-set-2` suffix**. Without it you get a 404.
- The **FRQ** file uses the short stem **`english-lit`**, while every other artifact spells out **`english-literature`**.
- Both of these constructed names were tried and **both returned 404**: `ap25-sg-english-literature.pdf`, `ap24-sg-english-literature.pdf`.

This is the **same class of trap** already documented on the CSA side, where the 2024/2023 question papers are `ap24-frq-comp-sci-a.pdf` rather than `…-computer-science-a.pdf` (see `../../ap_csa/ap_csa_exam/reference/question-sources-and-access.md`). **The rule for this whole repo: a 404 on a URL you built yourself means your guess was wrong, not that the document is missing.** Go to AP Central and click through.

## Bonus: your grammar work here also counts on the SAT

**Six of the thirteen sections in `../guides/grammar.md` test rules the SAT tests too** — commas & comma splices, parallel structure, semicolons & colons, subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, and verb-tense consistency. The SAT-side version of the same rules, written in SAT question format, is [`../../sat/reference/grammar-cheatsheet.md`](../../sat/reference/grammar-cheatsheet.md), and Erica Meltzer's free rules page ([thecriticalreader.com/complete-sat-grammar-rules/](https://thecriticalreader.com/complete-sat-grammar-rules/), digital-SAT-updated, verified 12 August 2026) covers exactly this ground.

**This is the only work in the repo that pays twice, so it's worth knowing about:** English grammar exams are *cumulative and continuous* all year, while the SAT side has a hard **6 March 2027** deadline. Learn each rule once; collect on it in both places.

One direction only, though: **close reading builds SAT Reading, not the reverse.** Working through *Julius Caesar* on the Folger text is real practice for SAT Craft & Structure inference. Going the other way is weak — SAT passages are a few sentences long, and nothing on the SAT asks you to sustain an argument across five paragraphs, which is what Mrs. P's essays are actually about.
